Where everyone is, and where the hands point

The same log-distance mapping the clock runs on: distance from home in kilometres, through log10, onto 0–360° clockwise from 12. Move anyone and watch their hand travel — at the real 90°/s, by the shortest path, the way the firmware drives it.

Brass ticks outside the ring mark 1, 10, 100, 1k and 10k kilometres — each decade is 83.7°. 12 o'clock is home; one full turn is 20 015 km, the far side of the Earth.

The six hands

Hub level sets the hand: level 1 is the front motor with the shortest hand, level 6 the longest. Pick a place, type coordinates, or drag the distance slider to fake a distance with no real position behind it.

The mapping, exactly as the clock computes it

d = max(haversine(home, person), 1.0) # km, R = 6371.0 angle = log10(d) / log10(pi * 6371.0) * 360.0 # deg clockwise from 12

So 1 km or less is 12 o'clock, 141 km is 6 o'clock, and the far side of the Earth is a full turn back to 12. Each decade of distance is 83.7°: the dial spends as much room on the first 10 km as on the last 10,000.
