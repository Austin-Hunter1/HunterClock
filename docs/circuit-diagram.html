Weasley Clock Circuit Diagram

Electronics Reference · Rev. 2

Weasley Clock — Circuit Diagram

Pin-to-pin wiring for the controller, the six motor drivers, and the six display nodes. Every wire names a physical pin on both ends. Companion to the hardware plan.

What changed from Rev. 1
  • RESET has to be wired. On the A4988 carrier the RST pin floats, and Pololu's page says to tie it to the adjacent SLP pin to enable the board. Rev. 1 said most boards come pre-jumpered, which was wrong. RESET and SLEEP now both go to 3V3.
  • The 12 V return joins logic ground. STEP and DIR are read against the driver's ground, so the motor supply's negative lead lands on the same star point as the Pico's GND. Rev. 1 said it did not need to.
  • Capacitors added. Pololu calls for at least 47 µF across VMOT and GND on each driver. That is six 100 µF caps at the drivers plus a bulk cap at the supply. The bulk cap is five more 100 µF caps in parallel (about 500 µF) from the same 20-pack, so no separate 470 µF part is needed.
  • Pin numbers checked against the Pico pinout reference and Pololu's A4988 page (sources at the bottom). Rev. 1 said they were checked, but they had only been drawn from memory.

Diagram 1 · Power

Three separate supplies, one star ground

The motor rail can pull real current when several steppers move at once, so it never shares a supply with the Pi or the displays.

Wires connect only where there is a dot. A wire crossing another without a dot is not connected.

The A4988 needs 8–35 V on VMOT to run, which is why the motor supply is 12 V and not 5 V. The supply's plug goes into a 2.1 × 5.5 mm female barrel-jack adapter, and its two screw terminals feed the +12V and ground buses (confirm the plug is center-positive before wiring). The 12 V supply's negative lead, the Pico's GND (pin 38), and every driver's GND pins all meet at one point. The Pi never needs its own ground wire to the motor side, because it reaches the Pico over USB. The display group is a separate 5 V domain with no wire to the rest.

Diagram 3 · One driver in full

All 16 A4988 pins, plus the motor

Shown for channel 1. Channels 2 to 6 repeat this exactly, with their own STEP and DIR pins.

Pin order matches the Pololu carrier: control row on one edge, power and motor row on the other. Clone boards use the same order, so check the silkscreen.

Motor leads Both motor types use the same lead colors in their datasheets: RED and RED/WHT are one coil, GRN/WHT and GRN are the other. Wire RED to 1A, RED/WHT to 1B, GRN/WHT to 2A, GRN to 2B. Confirm with a meter before powering: the two leads of one coil read about 1.5 Ω to each other and open to the other coil. Swapping the two wires of one coil only reverses rotation, and the firmware can flip DIR instead.

Set the current limit before connecting a motor

The A4988 limits coil current with the trim pot: Imax = Vref / (8 × Rs). Read the sense resistor marking on your boards (R100, R068 or R050), then measure Vref between the pot's metal screw and GND with logic power on and the motor disconnected. A clock hand needs little torque, so start near 1 A, which a driver handles without a heatsink. The Photect boards come with heat sinks, so stick one on each driver anyway. Also confirm each board is marked A4988: a DRV8825 uses Imax = 2 × Vref and labels the microstep pins M0 to M2, though the wiring here is otherwise the same.

Diagram 4 · Display node

ESP32 to GC9A01, one of six

Each node is a self-contained pair with its own ESP32, display, and MQTT topic. Nothing links the six nodes except WiFi.

Display pins are drawn grouped for clarity. On the real module, match wires by the printed label, since header order differs between makers.

Use the same colors on all six nodes, so a swapped wire is easy to spot. The dupont wires in the AITRIP packs cover all six nodes; they need to be female-to-female, since both the ESP32 header and the display header are pins.

Two things about the ESP32 GPIO2 and GPIO5 are boot strapping pins: GPIO2 has to be low at boot and GPIO5 high. The display inputs are high impedance, so this wiring boots normally, but if an upload ever fails, unplug the DC wire (GPIO2) while flashing. The plan uses the Aideepen 30-pin board with a micro-USB port and a CP2102 USB-serial chip (per the cart listing), which Windows normally installs a driver for by itself. On the common 30-pin layout, GPIO23, 18, 5, 4 and 2 plus 3V3 and a GND all sit on the same header, but I couldn't see this exact board's pinout, so find each pin by its printed label, not its position.

First power-up

Bring it up in four steps

  1. Wire the logic side only: Pico, 3V3 bus, GND, the MS/RESET/SLEEP bridges, STEP, DIR and ENABLE. No 12 V and no motors. Check for 3.3 V on every VDD pin.
  2. With logic power on and the motor supply still off, set Vref on each driver using the table above.
  3. Power off. Fit the capacitors (long leg to +12V, and check the stripe marking; the bulk cap is five 100 µF caps with all their + legs together), then connect 12 V with the motors still unplugged. Confirm 12 V at each VMOT and that nothing gets warm.
  4. Power off. Connect one motor, power on, and send a few steps. Add the rest one at a time. Never plug or unplug motor leads while the 12 V is on, because that can destroy the driver.

Sources

Pin tables were checked against pico.pinout.xyz, Pololu's A4988 carrier page (RST floating, VMOT and VDD ranges, 47 µF capacitor, current formula), and the ESP32 strapping-pin guides at espboards.dev and Last Minute Engineers. Motor lead colors and coil resistances come from the StepperOnline datasheets for the 17HS13-1504H and 23HS18-2004H.
